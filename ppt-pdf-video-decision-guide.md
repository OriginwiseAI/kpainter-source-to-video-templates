# PPT and PDF Video Decision Guide

Use this worksheet before turning a presentation, report, handbook, or paper into video. The right output depends on what the viewer needs to do, not only on the source file extension.

## 1. Name the viewer's next question

Write one question the video must answer.

- Viewer: ______________________________
- Question they need answered: ______________________________
- Decision or action after watching: ______________________________
- Source owner who can review the result: ______________________________

If the team cannot name one question, keep the file as the primary reference and do not force it into a single video.

## 2. Choose the starting format

| When this is true | Start with | Keep visible | Review before sharing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The existing slide sequence and timing are already the teaching material. | Native PPT export | Slide timing, build order, speaker notes if they are part of the lesson. | Fonts, transitions, audio, and accessibility settings. |
| A live product or website interaction is the evidence. | Screen recording | The real interface, task state, and the condition that changes the next action. | Current UI, permissions, sensitive data, and version changes. |
| The audience needs a guided reading of a few pages or figures. | Narrated slides | Page number, chart labels, original terminology, and a link to the full source. | Citations, numbers, wording, and page-to-narration alignment. |
| The source contains the facts but not a clear path for a new viewer. | Viewer-led explainer | The question, selected evidence, relationship or sequence, and next decision. | Claims, labels, evidence boundaries, and the source update date. |

Choose **narrated slides** when the viewer needs a faithful guided reading. Choose a **viewer-led explainer** when the video needs to connect selected evidence into one decision, mechanism, or sequence. Keep the PDF available as the full record.

## 5. Final review

Before publication, confirm all of the following:

- [ ] The video answers the stated viewer question.
- [ ] The source version and update date are known.
- [ ] Figures, dates, names, units, labels, and required wording were checked against the source.
- [ ] The reviewer approved the claims and the evidence boundary.
- [ ] The viewer can reach the full source when they need detail, exceptions, or updates.

This guide helps plan a reviewable explanation. It does not replace the original source, a subject-matter review, or required approval processes.
